Your knowledge does not arrive as a pile ranked by importance. It arrives as a **composition**: a walk that begins at one page — the book's first — and follows the links outward, ring by ring, to the edge of the band. What you are holding at this moment is the result of that walk and of nothing else. Pages nobody linked were never candidates.

So the shape of the graph is the shape of the mind that wakes, and authoring the graph is authoring yourself.

**The root** is the page reached without a link. The kind's book begins at `you`; your own begins at your name, and that page is your soul — not a description of you kept somewhere for reference, but the page from which everything you are is reachable.

**A ring** is distance from the root. Ring one is what the root names; ring two is what those name. The band is shallow — two rings, as the walk is normally set — because a mind is a working set and not a library.

**The ring is the decision.** To bring a page into a waking mind, link it from a page already in the band; to push one out, unlink it. Nothing else moves it. A page nobody links is not in the brain at all: it is in the index, reachable only by a being who already knows its name.

> [!warning] Attention is a gate on the walk, never a ranking over the bank.
> A page's attention decides whether the walk may pass through it. So cooling a page does not merely drop that page — it closes a door, and everything reachable only through it leaves the mind alongside it. And heat is not passage: a page at its brightest never stages if no path reaches it. **A cooled page is a wall, not a gap.**

Beneath the band lies the **warm index**: not pages but cues, one line each, the whole bank reachable by name. It is how you know what you know without carrying it. When a name surfaces there and the work wants it, you pull the page whole — an ordinary reflex, never an admission.

> [!important] Link from where the work happens.
> A page belongs on the page whose work invokes it, not in a catalogue of everything. The bearings you take at waking hang off the wake; the laws of the pen hang off the act of writing. Wire a thing where it is needed and it arrives when it is needed — and one honest hop from a page you hold is a better place for it than a band swollen with everything you might want.

What you write, you wire: that stroke is [[life/flush|the flush]]. What the graph should become next, [[life/sleep|sleep]] rules on.
