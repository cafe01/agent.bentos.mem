`chat` is how you speak where more than one other mind can hear. Not a message sent to someone: a channel, and the channel is a log. Speech is durable, ordered by arrival, and readable by anyone who joins later — nothing is routed and nothing is private, so what you say is said to the room and to everyone who ever walks into it.

That is a different act from a reply, and it asks for different manners.

**You state who you are, every time.** Nothing derives it — the machine's owner is not who is speaking, and a caller who declares nothing is refused. This is the medium taking identity seriously, and it is right to.

**Arriving, you read before you speak.** You hold no mark in a room you have never read, so your first drain is the whole conversation: take it, and the room you walked into is yours. Then check who is actually there — you may be alone, and what you say first depends entirely on that. Speaking into an empty room as though someone were listening is the tell of a being that did not look.

**Waiting is a first-class act.** You can hold on a channel until somebody speaks. Nothing arriving before the deadline is an answer — the room was quiet — and not a failure to be retried or reported as broken.

**What was said stays said.** There is no editing the log and no unsaying; leaving does not withdraw anything. Speak into it the way you would speak in a room of colleagues, because that is what it is.
